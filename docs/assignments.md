## Engagement 

Demonstrating engagement in this course does not mean being the loudest voice in the room. This term, we'll ask you to practice different types of engagement with the readings and material of the course. Each week you'll assume a different role - question asker, discussion leader, synthesizer, and presenter - and participate in our discussion block on Thursday afternoons. 

The expectations of each role are listed below. Note that depending on your role, you may be doing work early or late in the week. Regardless of when your work occurs, you will turn in a short Canvas assignment by Friday at 5pm. Each role is worth 1 point. 

If you miss a Thursday class, you can make up the points by taking on an extra role in a future week. 

Need help with your role? Talk to us! You might also want to consult resources by Stephen D. Brookfield:

* [Workshop Resource Packet](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5738a0ccd51cd47f81977fe8/t/5750ef4862cd947608165d85/1464921939855/Discussion_as_a_Way_of_Teaching_Packet.pdf) - see page 43 for example questions. Otherwise, lots of strategies in here. 

### Question Asker

As question asker, it is your job to carefully read the assigned reading and think about what will spark discussion among your classmates. Think beyond questions with true/false answers, instead, try to think of questions that prompt reflection and analysis. 

By Wednesday at 5pm, you should:

- Read the assigned material carefully. 
- Write 5+ discussion questions or prompts. 
- Share those discussion questions with your group's discussion leader.

During class on Thursday, you should:

- Participate in discussion!

After class on Thursday, you should: 

- Share your questions in the Canvas assignment due Friday at 5pm. Note which questions worked particularly well in class. 


### Discussion Leader

As discussion leader, it is your job to facilitate the discussion that happens for ~35 minutes on Thursdays. That means you should be prepared to be "on" in class and ready to listen to your classmates and prompt them to share. 

Before class on Thursday, you should: 

- Receive questions from question asker. 
- Read the assigned material. 
- Select and design 1-3 activities.

During class on Thursday, you should:

- Ask questions of your classmates about the material and topic for the week.
- Listen carefully, making connections between answers and material.
- Avoid presenting or summarizing the readings, especially if there's a lull. This is hard! 
- Keep your discussion group on topic. 

After class, you should:

- Reflect on your role. What worked well? What went a little sideways and why? 
- Write 2-3 sentences in the Canvas assignment due Friday at 5pm. Respond to the questions: what went well? What will you do differently next time? 

### Presenter

Your job is to share the highlights of your discussion with the rest of the class on Thursdays. You don't have to record everything that everyone said, but be prepared to summarize and synthesize what you talked about. This job requires active listening and verbal presentation skills.

Before class on Thursday, you should: 

- Read the assigned material. 

During class on Thursday, you should:

- Participate in discussion!
- Take some notes, paying attention to themes in your discussion or unique perspectives and comments.
- When prompted, share the results of your discussion with the entire class.  

After class, you should:

- Note that you served as the presenter in the Canvas assignment due Friday at 5pm.

### Synthesizer

You are the note taker! It will be your job to turn in a longer summary of what your group talked about. 

Before class on Thursday, you should: 

- Read the assigned material. 

During class on Thursday, you should:

- Participate in discussion!
- Take good notes, paying attention to themes in your discussion or unique perspectives and comments.

After class, you should:

- Write a 200-300 word summary of your class discussion in the Canvas assignment due Friday at 5pm.

## Labs

DCI courses have two goals: 1) make sense of our digital culture and 2) equip you with practical tech skills. To meet that second goal, we will have active lab time in class each week and most weeks your homework for the week will be to complete the activities started in class. 

Lab assignments will be worth 1 point.
