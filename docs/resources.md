# Resources

## Examples of Research on Internet and Social Media Culture
 
These are real studies of the internet and how it shapes behavior. You are not expected to produce anything like their scale. Read them to see how researchers pick an object of study, decide what counts as data, explain their method, and admit their limits. Those are the same moves your prospectus asks for.
 
What to pay attention to: Start with the abstract, skim the introduction, then find the data and methods section and ask:
 
* What did they actually collect? (How many comments, posts, videos, or people? From where, and when?)
* How did they decide what counts? (Categories, codes, or interview questions)
* What did they find and what couldn't they find?

**A note before you start:** Many of these papers are complex. They were written by professional researchers for other researchers, often with training in statistics, programming, or theory that you don't have yet, and that's fine. You are not expected to understand every paper, and you don't need to follow the methods or the math. Use them for ideas and framing: what questions people ask about the internet, what counts as data, how they explain what they did, etc.

### "TikTok, Do Your Thing": User Reactions to Social Surveillance in the Public Sphere

Authors: Meira Gilbert, Miranda Wei, Lindah Kotut - [Link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.20884)

* Hand-coding comments and videos about a TikTok trend where users film strangers in public and crowdsource identifying them
* Collected 60 videos and 1,901 comments
* You did this on a smaller scale in the week 6 lab

### Recommending Toxicity: The role of algorithmic recommender functions on YouTube Shorts and TikTok in promoting male supremacist influencers 

Author: Kendra Calhoun - [Link](https://www.jbe-platform.com/content/journals/10.1075/ip.00143.cal)
 
* Hand-coding TikToks and their comments to see how users refer back to the defunct app Vine and describe videos as having "Vine energy"
* Collected 201 TikToks and their comments
* A good example of a small sample and a playful topic that still makes an argument

### Recommending Toxicity: The role of algorithmic recommender functions on YouTube Shorts and TikTok in promoting male supremacist influencers
 
Authors: Catherine Baker, Debbie Ging, Maja Brandt Andreasen - [Link](https://www.dcu.ie/antibullyingcentre/recommending-toxicity-summary-report)
 
* An audit of whether TikTok and YouTube Shorts steer young men toward misogynistic "manosphere" content, using 10 test accounts on new phones
* Watched and hand-coded over 29 hours of recommended videos
* Shows how researchers study a personalized feed and it is upfront about its small sample, similar to your week 6 homework


### Algorithmic Folk Theories and Identity: How TikTok Users Co-Produce Knowledge of Identity and Engage in Algorithmic Resistance
 
Authors: Nadia Karizat, Dan Delmonaco, Motahhare Eslami, Nazanin Andalibi - [Link](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3476046)
 
* Interviews about how TikTok users think the For You Page works and how those beliefs change what they post
* Interviewed 15 US-based TikTok users
* Example of how to collect data about the internet and social media through interviews rather than comments and the like
